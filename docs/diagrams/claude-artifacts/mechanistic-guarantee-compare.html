Agent Control Plane — Governance Model, Today vs. Goal

Shrink the probabilistic surface

Almost everywhere in an agent's action space, behavior is probabilistic by default: it follows a skill or policy because it was asked to and chose to, not because anything stops it from doing otherwise. A mechanistic guarantee is the rare exception — a hook, or any future structural check, that holds regardless of what the agent infers. Left is how much of that space this repository actually locks down today. Right is the goal: expand mechanistic guarantees across more of that probabilistic space, without pretending judgment calls like was this authorized can be mechanized away.

What closes the gap between the two panels

  1. Role charters — bind identity, not just document it
    Today a charter states who's accountable in prose. A mechanized version would tie a charter to something checkable at runtime — a required approver on the record, not just named in a document.
  2. Execution policies — mechanize the checkable slice
    Authorization ("did the user actually say this") stays a judgment call. But evidence presence — a linked pull request, a citation, a re-read confirmation — is structurally checkable, and could gate completion the way a hook gates a commit.
  3. Leave the outer ring alone
    Instructions and skills exist precisely because not everything should be mechanized. The goal shrinks the gap already identified for policies and charters — it doesn't erase the interpretive layer.
